Mercury is the smallest planet in the solar system and the closest to the Sun.
Despite being closest to the Sun, Mercury is not the hottest planet.
Venus has a thick atmosphere that traps heat, making it the hottest planet in our solar system.
Earth is the third planet from the Sun and the only known planet to support life.
Mars is often called the "Red Planet" due to its reddish appearance from iron oxide.
Jupiter is the largest planet in the solar system and is known for its Great Red Spot.
Saturn is famous for its extensive ring system made mostly of ice particles and dust.
Uranus rotates on its side and has faint rings and a bluish color due to methane in its atmosphere.
Neptune is the most distant planet from the Sun and has the strongest winds in the solar system.
Pluto was reclassified from a planet to a dwarf planet in 2006 by the IAU.
The Moon is Earth's only natural satellite and influences tides due to its gravitational pull.
The Sun is a medium-sized yellow star at the center of our solar system.
Comets are icy bodies that release gas and dust, forming tails when near the Sun.
Asteroids are rocky bodies that mostly orbit in the asteroid belt between Mars and Jupiter.
The Kuiper Belt is a region beyond Neptune filled with icy bodies and dwarf planets.
The Oort Cloud is a theoretical cloud of icy objects that exists in the farthest reaches of the solar system.
Solar eclipses occur when the Moon passes between Earth and the Sun, casting a shadow on Earth.
Lunar eclipses happen when Earth passes between the Sun and the Moon, blocking sunlight from reaching the Moon.
Space probes have visited every planet in the solar system and provided detailed data and images.
Telescopes in space and on Earth allow us to observe distant galaxies and stars.
Black holes are regions in space with gravitational fields so strong that not even light can escape.
